Course Overview

Household and close contacts of people diagnosed with infectious tuberculosis face a substantially elevated risk of TB infection and progression to active disease, particularly children under five and people living with HIV. Proactive source and contact investigation breaks transmission chains early and finds people with TB who might otherwise be missed by passive facility-based care.

This intermediate course walks health workers and National TB Programme teams through the end-to-end contact investigation workflow aligned with WHO recommendations. Participants learn how to define index cases, prioritize household and congregate-setting contacts, conduct symptom and chest X-ray screening, and coordinate timely diagnostic evaluation.

Special attention is given to reverse contact (source) tracing for children diagnosed with TB, ethical and stigma-sensitive interviewing, and linking eligible contacts to TB preventive treatment (TPT) once active disease is ruled out.

By combining operational guidance with practical case scenarios, learners gain the skills needed to strengthen contact tracing coverage, documentation, and follow-up within routine TB services.

What you'll gain

  • Identify and prioritize high-risk household and close contacts
  • Apply source investigation protocols for paediatric TB cases
  • Conduct stigma-sensitive interviewing and home visits
  • Initiate and monitor TB preventive treatment (TPT)

Target audience & background

Target audience

Community health workers, TB nurses, physicians, and National TB Programme coordinators responsible for contact tracing, active case finding, and TB preventive treatment delivery.

Recommended background

Basic familiarity with tuberculosis transmission, clinical screening, and routine TB programme reporting workflows.

Learning outcomes

By the end of this course, learners will be able to:

  • Define index patients and prioritize household, close, and congregate-setting contacts for systematic TB screening
  • Conduct ethical, confidential, and stigma-sensitive contact interviews during facility visits and household outreach
  • Apply clinical and radiological screening algorithms to rule out active TB and refer presumptive cases for rapid molecular testing
  • Initiate eligible contacts on TB preventive treatment (TPT) and monitor adherence, outcomes, and contact registers
