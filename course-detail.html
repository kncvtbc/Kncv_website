Course Overview

Tuberculosis is a major cause of illness and death in children. In 2022, 1.25 million children and young adolescents fell ill with TB and 214,000 died, most of them under the age of five. Diagnosing TB in children is difficult: their symptoms overlap with common childhood illnesses, they carry low bacterial loads, and young children swallow their sputum instead of coughing it up. Collecting respiratory samples often means painful, invasive hospital procedures such as gastric aspiration or sputum induction.

Stool offers a simple, non-invasive alternative. Because TB bacteria from the lungs are swallowed and pass through the digestive tract, they can be detected in stool, and WHO recommends Xpert MTB/RIF and Xpert Ultra testing on stool as an initial diagnostic test for children. The Simple One-Step (SOS) stool method, developed by Petra de Haas and KNCV Tuberculosis Foundation, prepares stool for testing in a single step inside the standard Xpert Sample Reagent bottle. It runs on existing GeneXpert machines and supplies, so no extra laboratory equipment is needed.

This course is designed for the laboratory technicians who perform the test and for the clinicians and health workers who request it and act on the results. The first part of the course covers the burden of childhood TB, WHO diagnostic recommendations, and how stool samples are collected, stored and transported. The second part covers the principles of the SOS processing method, biosafety precautions, and hands-on practical training, ending with a video-based proficiency assessment reviewed by experts.

On completing the course, learners will be able to bring WHO-recommended, non-invasive childhood TB diagnosis to any GeneXpert site with confidence, and will understand why each step of the method matters so errors and invalid results stay low.

Target audience & background

Target audience

Laboratory technicians who perform Xpert MTB/RIF or Xpert Ultra testing and will process stool samples using the SOS method, as well as clinicians and health workers who request the test and act on results.

Recommended background

Laboratory technicians should be familiar with the Xpert MTB/RIF and Xpert Ultra assays.

Learning outcomes

By the end of this course, learners will be able to:

  • Explain the epidemiology of childhood TB, how GeneXpert testing applies to children, and the WHO recommendations for using stool as a primary diagnostic sample
  • Apply the right safety and quality precautions when collecting, storing, transporting and handling stool samples on an open laboratory bench
  • Assess stool consistency using the Bristol Stool Chart, select the correct amount of solid or liquid stool, and perform the SOS stool processing method
  • Interpret and report GeneXpert stool test results, including MTB Trace, and resolve invalid or error results
